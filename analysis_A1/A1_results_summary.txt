Cross-linguistic Calibration Analysis A1: DAT-RU vs English DAT
================================================================

Question: Is the ~11-point gap (RU ~89 vs EN ~78 mean) a property of
Russian language/culture, or an artifact of different embedding models?

Method:
- Top 200 words from DAT-RU dataset translated to English
- 195 valid word pairs (in both navec and GloVe vocabularies)
- Pairwise cosine distances computed in both embedding spaces
- 1000 random 7-word DAT scores simulated with identical word sets

Key Results:
-----------
1. DISTANCE CORRELATION (pairwise, 18,915 pairs):
   Pearson r  = 0.664
   Spearman ρ = 0.632
   R² = 0.440

   Interpretation: MODERATE correlation. The models agree on broad
   semantic structure but disagree substantially on many specific pairs.
   ~44% of variance in Russian distances is explained by English distances.

2. DISTANCE CALIBRATION:
   EN mean distance: 0.886
   RU mean distance: 0.906
   Ratio (RU/EN): 1.023 (Russian is ~2.3% larger on average)
   Linear mapping: RU = 0.728 * EN + 0.261

   The calibration curve shows navec compresses close pairs upward
   (navec shows closer words as less close) and slightly compresses
   distant pairs downward. Net effect is a small systematic shift.

3. DAT SCORE SIMULATION (same words, different models):
   EN mean score: 88.70 ± 3.62
   RU mean score: 90.70 ± 3.69
   Difference: +2.00 points (Cohen's d = 0.72, p < 1e-91)
   Score correlation: r = 0.708

   CRITICAL FINDING: Same words scored in GloVe yield mean ~88.7,
   not ~78. This means the ~78 English DAT mean reflects WORD CHOICE
   by English speakers, not the embedding model.

   The model calibration accounts for only ~2 points of the 11-point gap.

4. POLYSEMY ARTIFACTS (top disagreement pairs):
   - apple/computer: GloVe close (Apple Inc.), navec distant (just fruit)
   - river/fork: GloVe close (fork in a river), navec distant (utensil)
   - salt/lake: GloVe close (salt lake), navec distant
   - furniture cluster: navec treats стол-шкаф-диван as very close

   These reflect genuine semantic differences between languages/corpora.

Conclusion:
----------
The ~11-point gap between DAT-RU (~89) and English DAT (~78) is
MOSTLY NOT a calibration artifact:
  - ~2 points: model calibration (navec vs GloVe systematic offset)
  - ~9 points: word selection differences between Russian and English speakers

Russian speakers choose words that are inherently more distant from each
other (in BOTH embedding spaces), suggesting either:
  a) Different word-selection strategies (Russian speakers use more diverse
     semantic categories)
  b) Sample characteristics (DAT-RU participants may differ demographically)
  c) Russian morphology/vocabulary naturally affords greater semantic spread

The moderate correlation (r=0.66) between models means cross-linguistic
score comparison should be done cautiously, but the direction is clear:
Russian speakers genuinely score higher, and this is not primarily an
embedding artifact.
